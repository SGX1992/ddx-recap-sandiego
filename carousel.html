Takeaway 1 of 5

Good is free now. Great is the job.

When everyone can clear the bar for good, taste, judgment and a durable point of view are what separate teams. AI didn’t change design; it exposed how shallow most of it was.

Richard Ward, Otter.ai · Kevin Wong, Webflow · Jaleesa Chagan, GAIN
2026 evidence
91%
of designers now use AI for design tasks every week, up from 54% a year ago.
Designer Fund, AI in Design 2026

Takeaway 2 of 5

Research stops being a project and becomes a pulse.

When teams ship weekly, episodic studies arrive after the decision. TaylorMade’s AI caddie showed the alternative: research before, A/B tests during, evaluation after, always on.

Prayag Narula, Marvin · Harriet Swan, GAIN · Katie Niles, TaylorMade
2026 evidence

Takeaway 3 of 5

Stop designing screens. Start designing decisions.

Design’s value is measured by its influence on what gets built, not by deliverables. The next brief is the organisation itself: its systems, ownership and invisible labour.

Chris Gielow, UX Speakeasy · Twisha Shah-Brandenburg, Target · Diana Tobey, IDEO
2026 evidence
21 → 41%
of designers now do development work, doubling in a year. The roles converge on the decision.
Figma, 2026 AI Report

Takeaway 4 of 5

The human stays irrational, and irreplaceable.

The rational user most products are designed for doesn’t exist. AI can read the deck; it can’t read the room. As machines generate more, human originality becomes the scarce resource.

Don Norman · Rowan Salama, Careem · Stephanie Mencarelli, Adobe · Kara Fitzpatrick
2026 evidence
50%
of US consumers prefer brands that don’t use generative AI in customer-facing content.

Takeaway 5 of 5

Context is the asset. The experience is the business.

Models change weekly; structured knowledge compounds. And when an agent is the one browsing and buying, the digital experience is no longer a layer on the business. It is the business.

Diana Wolosin, Netflix · AJ Azzi, Askable · Scott Robinson, FreshForm · Ann Kostopanagiotou, Mondo Robot
